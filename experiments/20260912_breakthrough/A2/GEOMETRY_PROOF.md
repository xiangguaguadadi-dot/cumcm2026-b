# Common-rotation discovery certificate

The original 21 sites are exact six-decimal constants in C7. The original independent integer checker has been re-executed, using the original certificate SHA256 `8eaeae3c86cd7378ef4a242afdae2ba35d3620ba0c82cf9419ab5ebdb4cdeaba`. It confirms 11,200 disjoint quadtree leaves, of which 5,796 cover the disk and 5,404 are outside. Across covered leaves its minimum range slack is 0.1096134294 m and minimum supporting-hull slack is 0.0007287540 m.

For every source point x in the 1800 m disk, the original certificate supplies sites P(x), all strictly within 1000 m of x, such that x belongs to conv(P(x)). If R is any common orthogonal rotation, write y=R x. Norms, convex combinations, and closed half-planes are preserved: every R p stays within 1000 m of y and y belongs to conv(R P(x)). Any closed emission half-plane through y must contain at least one of those sites (otherwise their convex combination would have strictly negative normal projection). The disk itself is rotation invariant. Thus the whole continuum of source positions and antenna orientations remains covered. The old axis-aligned quadtree is not itself reused as an axis-aligned certificate: its cells and witnesses are transported by the mathematical proof.

Implementation selects an angle once, immediately after the real origin scan. At that point no non-origin station has been visited; an assertion checks every channel's recorded station set. Site zero remains exactly the origin. Future scan IDs therefore refer consistently to the new actual coordinates. Every unknown-channel measurement at a future site is still paid for and recorded; neither an estimated source center nor a planning quadrature counts as an observation. Actual source clearing still requires the existing polygon/optical mechanisms and successful clear. Final exit still requires all sites scanned on unresolved channels, or the official 16-source upper bound.

For finite-precision sites, each canonical coordinate has magnitude <=1864 m. Binary64 sin/cos and two-product rotation errors are orders of magnitude below the 0.000728 m strict original hull margin. The targeted check transports every covered cell/witness through all 24 selected rotations and independently rechecks range and supporting-hull margins in binary64. This is a conservative numerical check supporting the analytic isometry argument, not a new exact-integer certificate for rotated floating-point sites.

The conditional-route bit masks are only a surrogate prior. Their canonical quadrature is transported together with the geometry rather than rebuilt on a fixed external angular mesh, so rotation does not manufacture a change in the assumed radial/angular prior. They never remove a true coverage requirement.
